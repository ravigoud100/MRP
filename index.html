AI Usage & Behavioral Patterns
How daily AI use shapes decision-making and autonomy
01 · Scatter
AI Prompts vs Decision Latency
Behavioral
Finding: Higher daily AI prompt frequency correlates with increased decision latency. Youth using AI heavily tend to pause longer before making independent choices — suggesting dependency-driven hesitation.
02 · Grouped Bar
Verification Behavior by AI Usage Level
Autonomy
Finding: Youth with heavy AI usage (>200 min/day) show measurably lower Verification Behavior Scores — indicating reduced critical evaluation and over-reliance on automated responses.
03 · Distribution
AI Usage by School Level
Distribution
Finding: AI prompt usage varies across school levels — College students show the widest distribution, suggesting divergent usage habits in higher education.
04 · Histogram
Automation Bias: Heavy vs Light Users
Cognitive Bias
Finding: Heavy AI users (>30 prompts/day) cluster at significantly higher automation bias values than light users, revealing a distinct behavioral divergence.
05 · Grouped Bar
Decision Confidence by Age Group
Development
Finding: Confidence in independent decisions is consistently lower for heavy AI users across all ages — the 13–15 cohort shows the steepest impact.
06 · Risk Scatter
Autonomy Risk Pattern Analysis
High Priority
Finding: A distinct high-risk cluster emerges among youth with high Automation Bias combined with low Verification Behavior Scores. These individuals also show higher Decision Latency and lower Confidence — a compounding pattern where AI over-reliance erodes both critical thinking and self-directed judgment. Point color = calculated risk score; point size = decision latency.

07 · Bubble
Screen Time · Attention · Fatigue
Multi-Dimensional

Clinical & Wellness Impact
Mental stress, fatigue, and cognitive health outcomes linked to AI dependency
⚕️

Case Concern: Youth Mental Health & Cognitive Development

The Eastbrook case explicitly raised concerns about youth fatigue, mental health, and cognitive wellbeing. The three visualizations below directly address these clinical dimensions using Fatigue_Level, Attention_Score, and Confidence variables — responding to the professor's feedback that clinical impact was absent from the solution.

09 · Clinical Bar
Fatigue Level by AI Usage
Clinical
Clinical Finding: Average fatigue rises significantly with AI usage intensity. Heavy users show measurably elevated fatigue — a key wellness indicator linked to cognitive depletion and reduced mental resilience.
10 · Clinical Scatter
Fatigue vs Attention Score
Cognitive Health
Clinical Finding: A clear inverse relationship exists between fatigue and attention. This pattern is most pronounced among heavy AI users — suggesting a cognitive depletion cycle driven by AI over-reliance.
11 · Radar
Wellness Profile by School Level
Wellness
Clinical Finding: The radar maps five wellness dimensions across school levels — revealing where clinical concerns are most concentrated and where the Youth Wellness Council should prioritize programs.
